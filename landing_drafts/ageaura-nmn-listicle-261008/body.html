7 Things the Research Says (and Doesn't Yet) About NMN and NAD+ After 40

By Dr. Marcus Hale | Wellness Nest editorial team

Last Updated Oct 8, 2026

Summary: NMN is one of the most talked-about longevity ingredients, and human trials are still catching up to the hype. Here is what the trials actually measured, what moved and what didn't, and why NMN is so often sold alongside TMG.

01

NMN is a building block, not NAD+ itself

NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) is a small molecule your body can convert into NAD+, a coenzyme every cell needs to make energy and repair DNA. Taking NMN is a bet that more of the building block means more of the finished coenzyme — which is testable, and several trials have tested exactly that.

It's a precursor claim, not a finished-product claim. The question worth asking is whether swallowing NMN actually raises NAD+ in people, and by how much.

02

In a two-month trial, NAD+ did rise

A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial gave 66 adults aged 40–65 a daily dose of NMN or a placebo for two months. The NMN group's NAD+/NADH ratio was up about 11% at day 30 and 38% at day 60; the placebo group showed no change at day 30 and a 14.3% rise by day 60.

That's the clearest part of the NMN story: oral NMN does change blood NAD+ markers in a controlled trial. What it means for how you feel day to day is a separate question — this trial measured a blood ratio, not energy, strength, or mood.

03

Older adults kept a quicker walking pace

A 12-week, placebo-controlled trial in older adults gave a daily amount of NMN against a dummy pill. The main test — a stepping drill — showed no difference between groups. But on a secondary measure, a short 4-meter walk, the NMN group was significantly faster than placebo by week 12, and blood NAD+ markers were higher too. The NMN group also scored better on a sleep-quality questionnaire at week 12.

Worth flagging plainly: the trial's own primary outcome (the stepping test) did not separate the groups. The walking-speed and sleep results are secondary findings in one small study, not a settled result.

04

The bigger picture is more mixed

A 2025 meta-analysis pooled a dozen randomized trials of people taking NMN. It confirmed the NAD+-raising effect holds up across studies — that part replicates. But most of the outcomes people actually care about, like fasting glucose and cholesterol, did not differ significantly between NMN and placebo groups.

The reviewers' own conclusion: an exaggeration of NMN's benefits may exist in the field, and more, larger trials are needed before claiming metabolic benefits as settled.

05

Why TMG so often rides along with NMN

The usual rationale: when the body turns over NAD+, the leftover nicotinamide is cleared partly by methylation — the walking trial above measured those methylated breakdown products (2-PY and 4-PY) rising with NMN (Morifuji et al., 2024). TMG (trimethylglycine, also called betaine) is a methyl donor: in healthy adults, daily betaine lowered fasting homocysteine, a methylation marker, within six weeks (Olthof et al., 2003).

So the case for pairing is a mechanistic one: TMG may help keep methylation supplied while NMN is in use. That reasoning has not itself been tested as a combined trial (see the next point).

06

No trial has tested NMN and TMG together

Here is the honest gap: every trial above tested NMN alone, or TMG alone. We found no published, peer-reviewed human trial that dosed NMN and TMG together as a single combined intervention and measured the outcome. The pairing is a formulation choice built on each ingredient's separate evidence, not a tested combination in its own right.

That doesn't make the pairing wrong — it makes it unproven as a pair, which is different from unproven at all. Worth knowing before you decide how much weight to put on “complex” formulas generally.

07

Whatever you choose, daily use is what the trials modeled

Every trial cited here (see the sources below) dosed participants every day for weeks or months straight. None tested occasional or “when I remember” use. If a NAD+ supplement sits unopened half the week, it isn't being used the way any of these trials actually measured it.

That makes the format you pick a practical question, not just a comfort one: whatever makes the daily habit easiest to keep is doing real work.

08
