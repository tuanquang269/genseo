5 Reasons Creatine Is Worth a Look After 40

By Dr. Marcus Hale | Wellness Nest editorial team
Last updated October 6, 2026

Summary: Creatine monohydrate is one of the most-researched supplements in sports nutrition. Here is what the studies found, what they did not test, and the one habit that decides whether it does anything for you.

01

It's one of the most-studied supplements in sports nutrition

The International Society of Sports Nutrition reviewed the creatine research and concluded that creatine monohydrate raises the creatine stored in muscle and supports high-intensity exercise and lean mass gains during training. The same review found that short- and long-term use appeared safe and well tolerated in healthy people (Kreider et al., 2017).

If you take medication or have a kidney condition, check with your doctor before you start.

Evidence: Position stand, J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017 (human research review)

02

It tops up the creatine stored in your muscles

In a classic study of 31 men, muscle creatine rose by about 20% after a six-day high-dose "loading" week. A group taking a small daily amount reached a similar 20% rise more gradually, over 28 days (Hultman et al., 1996).

That study used creatine powder and measured muscle creatine levels, not strength.

Evidence: Human trial, J Appl Physiol 1996

03

With strength training, it's linked to more lean mass in older adults

A meta-analysis of 22 randomized trials with 721 older adults (average ages 57 to 70) found that creatine plus resistance training produced about 1.37 kg more lean mass than training alone, along with greater gains in chest-press and leg-press strength (Chilibeck et al., 2017).

The training mattered: creatine was studied alongside lifting, not instead of it.

Evidence: Meta-analysis of randomized trials, Open Access J Sports Med 2017

04

You don't need a loading phase

Loading fills muscles faster, but a steady small daily amount gets you to a similar place within a few weeks (Antonio et al., 2021). We compare both approaches in our guide: Creatine gummies loading phase: is it needed?

Evidence: Research review, J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2021

05

It only works if you take it every day

In the same 1996 study, muscle creatine stayed up while people kept taking a small daily amount. When they stopped, it drifted back to where it started within about 30 days (Hultman et al., 1996).

So the real question isn't which week you start. It's whether you'll still be taking it in month three.

Evidence: Human trial, J Appl Physiol 1996
