01

A well-studied ingredient

The International Society of Sports Nutrition reviewed creatine monohydrate research and found that it raises muscle creatine stores and can support high-intensity exercise and lean mass gains during training.

That review also described creatine as generally well tolerated in healthy people. Have a medical condition or take medication? Ask your clinician first.

Position stand · Kreider et al., 2017

02

It tops up muscle stores

In a classic study of 31 men, muscle creatine rose about 20% after a high-dose loading week.

A lower daily amount reached a similar rise more gradually, over 28 days. The study measured muscle creatine, not strength.

Human study · Hultman et al., 1996

03

Training still does the work

In a meta-analysis of older adults, creatine paired with resistance training produced greater chest-press and leg-press strength gains than training alone.

The pattern matters: creatine was studied as a training partner, not as a substitute for lifting.

Meta-analysis · Chilibeck et al., 2017

04

Lean mass rose with lifting

The same meta-analysis found more lean tissue mass when creatine was added to resistance training.

Its participants were older adults, and the result came from structured training studies. It does not mean the supplement replaces progressive exercise.

05

Loading is optional

Loading fills muscle stores faster, but a steady daily routine reaches a similar level more gradually, according to the evidence review.

That gives you a choice of pace. The research does not make a loading phase a requirement.

Evidence review · Antonio et al., 2021

06

Consistency keeps stores up

In the 1996 study, muscle creatine stayed elevated while participants continued the routine.

After they stopped, levels moved back toward baseline in about 30 days. The practical lesson is less about a perfect start and more about a habit you can repeat.

Human study · Hultman et al., 1996

07

The format can help

The studies above used repeated routines, not occasional use. A supplement only fits the evidence if the habit fits your real week.

That makes convenience a practical question, not a performance claim: will you remember it on training days and rest days alike?
