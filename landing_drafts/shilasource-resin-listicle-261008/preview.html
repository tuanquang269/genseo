01

It's a resin from mountain rock

Shilajit is a dark, sticky, mineral-rich resin that seeps from mountain rock. It has a long history in Ayurvedic tradition.

A 2014 safety review names fulvic acid and dibenzo-α-pyrones as key compounds, and notes that well-controlled human studies are relatively few.

Safety review · Stohs, Phytother Res 2014

02

Men 45 to 55: testosterone rose

In a 90-day placebo-controlled trial, healthy men aged 45 to 55 took purified shilajit twice a day.

Total testosterone, free testosterone and DHEAS rose significantly versus placebo, while LH and FSH stayed steady. A purified extract, tested in men only.

Human RCT · Pandit et al., Andrologia 2016

03

Strength held up better

In an 8-week trial, 63 active men took a lower dose, a higher dose or placebo, then did a hard leg-fatigue test.

Among the stronger half, the higher-dose group lost 8.9% of strength versus 16.0% on placebo. The lower dose matched placebo.

Human RCT · Keller et al., JISSN 2019

04

Bone density in women 45 to 65

A 48-week trial followed 60 postmenopausal women with low bone density. They took a shilajit extract at one of two doses, or placebo.

Bone density kept falling on placebo, but the loss was smaller with the extract, more so at the higher dose. One trial, one specific extract.

Human RCT · Pingali & Nutalapati, 2022

05

Purity matters with resins

Ayurvedic products can carry heavy metals. A JAMA study tested 193 Ayurvedic products bought online: 20.7% contained lead, mercury or arsenic.

So ask any shilajit seller about lab testing for your batch, not just a general claim.

Product testing · Saper et al., JAMA 2008

06

The resin form is barely studied

The controlled trials above used purified, standardized extracts in capsules. Resin itself has far less evidence.

The one resin study we found was a 28-day pilot in 25 men with no placebo group. Promising, but not proof.

Literature check · resin: one open-label pilot only

07

How resin is usually taken

Resin is usually dissolved: a pea-size piece stirred into warm, not boiling, water or tea until no dark lumps remain.

The serving on your own jar's label is the one to use. Labels differ between brands.
