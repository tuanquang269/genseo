01

Monohydrate is the studied form

Most creatine research uses one form: creatine monohydrate. A 2021 expert review counts more than 500 peer-reviewed papers on creatine supplementation.

No other form has been shown to beat it. So the first word to look for on any label, powder or gummy, is monohydrate.

Expert review · Antonio et al., JISSN 2021

02

A small daily amount works too

You don't need a loading phase. In a classic muscle biopsy study, a high-dose load raised muscle creatine about 20% in 6 days.

A low daily dose reached the same 20% rise by day 28, just more slowly. Steady daily intake gets you there.

Human study · Hultman et al., J Appl Physiol 1996

03

Gummies were tested in a trial

Does creatine still work in a gummy? A 10-week randomized trial gave 32 female beach volleyball athletes creatine gummies daily or no supplement.

The gummy group jumped higher and changed direction faster. Lean mass did not differ between groups. One small trial, but a real one.

Randomized trial · Pereira et al., 2026

04

With training, more lean mass

A meta-analysis of 22 trials pooled 721 adults with average ages of 57 to 70. All did resistance training two to three times a week.

Adding creatine gave 1.37 kg more lean mass than training alone, plus stronger chest and leg presses. The key word is training.

Meta-analysis of 22 RCTs · Chilibeck et al., 2017

05

Women see small, real gains

Evidence in women has been thinner. A 2026 meta-analysis gathered 34 studies of women who exercise or train, with 692 participants.

It found small positive effects on strength and power. The authors rate certainty as low, so expect modest changes, not dramatic ones.

Meta-analysis (34 studies in women) · Lin et al., 2026

06

No head-to-head with powder

The gummy trial compared creatine gummies with no supplement. We found no published trial comparing gummies with powder directly.

So the case for gummies rests on the same monohydrate research, plus convenience. The amount per serving is what to check.

Literature check · no gummy vs powder trial found

07

Well tolerated in healthy adults

The ISSN position stand concludes that creatine is safe and well tolerated in healthy people, in studies lasting up to five years.

Taking medication or managing a kidney condition? Ask your doctor first. Daily use is what the research tested.

Position stand · Kreider et al., JISSN 2017
