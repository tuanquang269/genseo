## Do you swallow NMN capsules or dissolve them?

**Use the capsule as supplied unless a pharmacist or manufacturer confirms a different method.** General [NHS pill-swallowing guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/problems-swallowing-pills/) recommends water and advises against opening capsules or otherwise altering pills without medical advice. That is general swallowing guidance, not an AgeAura absorption study.

AgeAura's FAQ mentions taking the product with water when breaking a fast. It does not specify a water volume or describe a dissolve-under-the-tongue method. There is no reason to turn that wording into a measured-glass requirement or a claim that one delivery method works faster.

![AgeAura capsule bottle and a half-filled water glass beside a folded napkin on a worn wooden breakfast table](/Users/kevinkreative/genseo/.claude/worktrees/agent-ad679c8266152e949/tmp_images/how-to-take-nmn/water-routine.webp)

A practical sequence is simple:

1. Read your container's directions and warnings before the first use.
2. Set out only the labeled amount, with water available.
3. Follow the swallowing advice appropriate to you; do not improvise by crushing, chewing, or opening the capsule.
4. Mark the serving as taken, then put the bottle away according to its storage instructions.

If capsules are difficult to swallow, ask a pharmacist about an appropriate alternative rather than mixing their contents into food yourself. If you also have difficulty swallowing food or drink, the NHS advises against trying its pill-swallowing techniques because of choking risk. Seek medical advice instead.

For the separate clock-time question, see [when to take NMN: morning or night](https://wellnessnest.co/blogs/news/when-to-take-nmn-morning-or-night-us-2026). Changing the hour is not the same decision as changing the capsule.

## What if you miss a day of NMN?

**Do not invent a catch-up dose.** The checked AgeAura sources do not provide missed-day instructions, and the product FAQ says not to exceed the label. Ask the manufacturer or your pharmacist how to resume; a blank calendar square is not permission to double the next serving.

This is a limit on the advice available, not a claim that a missed day causes harm or erases a benefit. No missed-dose study is being used to promise either outcome. The same caution applies when you cannot remember whether you already took the capsules: check your record rather than guessing and adding more.
