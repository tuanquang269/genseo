## Can you take creatine without food?

**Food is not established as a requirement for creatine to increase muscle stores. That is different from proving every creatine gummy is suitable for every person before breakfast.** The most useful distinction is between research on the ingredient and directions for the finished product.

In a [human trial comparing creatine alone with creatine plus carbohydrate](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8944667/), muscle creatine increased in both groups. Adding carbohydrate produced a larger increase under the study conditions. The experiment therefore does not support the claim that creatine taken without added carbohydrate is wasted.

It also does not settle the exact question “can I take this gummy after an overnight fast?” The study used dissolved creatine, not FusiForce gummies. A creatine-alone treatment is not automatically evidence that participants ate nothing throughout the day. Avoid turning that distinction into a promise about fasted training, stomach comfort, or superior results.

For a practical decision, separate three questions: does the ingredient require carbohydrate, does your product require food, and does your chosen routine suit you? Those are related questions, not interchangeable ones.

## Does a meal change absorption or retention?

**The cited trials measured creatine accumulation or retention—not proof that breakfast increases intestinal absorption of gummies.** Absorption describes movement from the gut into the body; retention concerns how much stays in the body, while muscle accumulation concerns stores in that tissue.

The [carbohydrate trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8944667/) enrolled 24 men over five days. Both groups increased muscle creatine, with a larger increase in the carbohydrate group. Its substantial simple-carbohydrate regimen was a study protocol, not a recommendation to add a sugary drink to breakfast.

A [second trial examining protein and carbohydrate](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10956365/) studied 12 men and found enhanced whole-body creatine retention with certain carbohydrate-containing treatments. That is evidence about retention during the experimental supplementation period. It is not a direct comparison of ordinary breakfasts, and it does not establish better long-term training results from taking gummies with food.
