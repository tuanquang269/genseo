Shilajit and blood pressure deserve a medication review, not a self-treatment experiment. ## Does shilajit lower blood pressure in humans?

**The evidence reviewed here does not establish shilajit alone as a blood pressure treatment.** A study mentioning cardiovascular effects is not necessarily a trial of hypertension treatment in people. Check the organism, the formulation and the outcome before accepting a headline.

One frequently relevant paper studied the heart of **Daphnia**, an aquatic organism—not people with hypertension. Its researchers observed changes in heart rate at different concentrations. That is not a measurement of human blood pressure, and it supplies no safe human dose. See the [indexed study abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22529672).

A separate randomized study examined an **Arogyavardhini compound plus lifestyle modification** in people with metabolic syndrome. The abstract reported blood pressure changes, but the intervention was a compound alongside lifestyle changes, not a trial isolating this ShilaSource resin. Attributing the result to shilajit alone would erase that distinction. See the [clinical trial abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33281394).

Even a human shilajit trial can answer the wrong question for this reader. The [purified-shilajit testosterone study](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26395129) evaluated androgen-related outcomes in healthy volunteers. Its abstract does not establish safe co-use with your blood pressure prescription. A finding about one outcome cannot be repackaged as proof about another.

| Evidence you encounter | What it can tell you | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Daphnia heart study | Effects on heart rate in that experimental organism | A safe human blood pressure treatment |
| Compound plus lifestyle trial | Outcomes for that combined intervention | The independent effect of this resin |
| Healthy-volunteer hormone trial | Outcomes studied in those volunteers | Compatibility with your prescription |
| Product packaging | The identity and printed label statements | Clinical effectiveness for hypertension |

The practical gap is not whether someone has ever studied shilajit. It is whether the study actually answers your medication question. Do not turn an interesting mechanism, animal result or testimonial into permission to change treatment.

## Who should check with a pharmacist before starting?

**Anyone taking blood pressure medication should request an individual review before adding shilajit.** This is a precaution about supplement use, not a claim that every blood pressure drug has a proven shilajit interaction. [NIH's National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/using-dietary-supplements-wisely) warns that dietary supplements can interact with medicines and can pose risks with particular medical conditions.

Ask for that review whether your prescription is described as an ACE inhibitor, an ARB, a calcium-channel blocker, a beta blocker or a diuretic. Bring the actual medicine names rather than relying on the class alone. This list identifies what to discuss; it does not label a specific interaction as confirmed.

People with an existing medical condition, upcoming surgery or several medicines have additional reasons to discuss supplements with their care team. NCCIH also notes that many supplements have not been tested in pregnant or breastfeeding people. Avoid assuming that a natural origin makes a product suitable for every situation.
