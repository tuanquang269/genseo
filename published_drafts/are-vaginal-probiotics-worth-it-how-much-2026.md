## What a Star Rating Cannot Prove

A star average tells you how many people clicked a rating button, not what dose they took or whether the formula they reviewed matches what ships today. It can't show you:

- **The exact strain and CFU count** — only the Supplement Facts label does that.
- **Whether the sweetener is sugar-free** — FloraFresh is not; it uses maltitol and isomalt.
- **Whether the price or stock status changed since the review was posted** — only the live product page does that.
- **Whether the claims on the package are structure/function claims (not disease claims)** — the FDA requires a disclaimer line precisely because these statements are not independently verified before sale: the FDA does not test dietary supplements before they're sold, and does not pre-approve structure/function claims on a product label ([FDA Q&A on dietary supplements](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-consumers-using-dietary-supplements/questions-and-answers-dietary-supplements); [FDA structure/function claims page](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/structurefunction-claims)). That's not unique to FloraFresh — it's true of every supplement sold without a drug approval, and it's exactly why reading the label yourself matters more here than in most product categories.

None of this means FloraFresh doesn't work. It means a star rating is the wrong tool to answer "is it worth it," and the label plus the live offer are the right ones.
