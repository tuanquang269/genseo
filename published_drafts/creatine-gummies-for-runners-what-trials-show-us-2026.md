## What creatine can—and cannot—do for runners

Creatine helps replenish phosphocreatine, a rapid energy system used during brief, hard efforts. That makes the evidence more relevant to repeated intervals, hill surges, finishing kicks, and gym sessions than to an uninterrupted easy run. The International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand describes the strongest performance rationale around high-intensity and repetitive exercise, while evidence for prolonged steady-state endurance is less consistent ([Kreider et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/)).

## Does creatine help long-distance running?

Creatine has a clearer case for the repeated high-intensity parts of distance training than for steady-state distance itself. A 10-mile easy run uses a different demand pattern from eight 400-meter repeats, a hilly race, or a sprint finish. Reviews and position statements therefore support a cautious, individualized trial rather than a promise of faster marathon pace.

Use the following decision test:

- **Intervals, hills, or surges:** a reasonable situation to discuss a trial.
- **Strength training alongside running:** a stronger fit for creatine's established use case.
- **Only low-intensity steady running:** expect uncertain direct benefit.
- **A weight-sensitive race soon:** consider the scale-weight tradeoff before starting.

The result is not an anti-creatine message. It is a matching exercise: the supplement's best-supported role is repeated, forceful effort, while the runner's goal may be entirely aerobic.

## Will creatine water weight slow a runner down?

It can add scale weight, especially during a rapid loading phase, but the amount and running consequence vary. The ISSN position stand identifies weight gain as the most consistently reported effect and describes approximately 0.5–1.0 liters of short-term fluid retention during early loading, primarily associated with intracellular water ([Kreider et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/)). That is not the same as saying every runner will gain that amount or feel slower.

For a runner, extra mass matters more when the event involves sustained uphill running, heat, or a strict body-mass target. It may matter less when the added training quality, strength, or repeated-sprint capacity is the goal. Track body weight, easy-run feel, interval splits, and recovery separately for two to four weeks instead of judging the plan from one weigh-in.

Do not try to force rapid water loss through dehydration. Heat, sweat, and fluid needs vary by weather and person. A clinician or sports dietitian can help if a runner has a history of disordered eating, kidney disease, or a medically directed fluid restriction.

## Should runners load creatine?

Runners do not need to load creatine to use it. The position stand describes a faster saturation approach followed by a lower daily maintenance amount, plus a slower approach using a smaller daily amount over roughly four weeks ([Kreider et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/)). Loading can make short-term water gain more noticeable, so it may be a poor fit immediately before a race or during a period when a runner is monitoring body mass.
