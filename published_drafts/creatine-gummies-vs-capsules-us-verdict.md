## What does the safety evidence mean for your choice?

The safety evidence for studied creatine use does not make every supplement suitable for every person. Format is not a medical screening tool.

The [position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/) reports that research in healthy people did not substantiate commonly alleged kidney injury from studied creatine use. It also identifies increased body weight as a consistently observed effect. Neither finding establishes safety for someone with kidney disease or proves the contents of a specific gummy or capsule.

If you have kidney disease, are pregnant or breastfeeding, take medication, or have been told to limit supplements, discuss use with your clinician before choosing either format. A pleasant flavor does not remove the need for that conversation. Tell your clinician about supplements before interpreting bloodwork, rather than independently deciding that a result is harmless.

Keep expectations tied to your training goal. Evidence involving repeated high-intensity exercise is not a promise of an immediate energy boost, a treatment for fatigue or a guaranteed change in appearance. There is no reason to turn a format decision into a medical claim.
