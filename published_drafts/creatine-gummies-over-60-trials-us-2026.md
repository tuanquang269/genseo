## What did creatine trials in older adults actually find?

**Creatine plus resistance training can improve lean tissue mass and some strength measures compared with training alone. Results vary, and an average research benefit is not a guaranteed personal outcome.**

A [2017 meta-analysis by Chilibeck and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138605/) pooled 22 studies involving 721 participants. Mean ages across studies ranged from 57 to 70 years. Participants performed resistance training while receiving creatine or a placebo. The pooled difference favored creatine for lean tissue mass, chest-press strength, and leg-press strength.

That is relevant to creatine for seniors, but it needs boundaries. The review did not exclusively enroll people over 60. It did not establish that a particular gummy brand works. Some included interventions also used other nutritional supplements, which complicates a simple product-level conclusion.

More recent findings are not uniformly positive. A [2025 strength-and-power meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40944139/) found benefits across several outcomes overall, but older-adult subgroups showed smaller or nonsignificant changes in several strength measures. Different study selections and outcomes can produce different estimates. It is more accurate to describe a possible training aid than a reliable reversal of age-related muscle loss.

### Lean mass is not the same as everyday independence

A body-composition measurement, a stronger chest press, and being able to climb stairs comfortably are different outcomes. Do not assume that an improvement in a gym test proves fewer falls, treatment of sarcopenia, or restored independence. The cited reviews do not establish those promises for FusiForce gummies.

For an individual decision, ask which outcome matters: following a training program consistently, progressing an exercise safely, or making an ordinary task easier. Record that goal before buying a supplement so that a marketing claim does not become the measure of success.

## Does creatine help without resistance exercise?

**Creatine alone is not a substitute for a strength-training program. The evidence for substantial muscle and functional gains without training is much less convincing.**

The [2021 evidence review on common creatine questions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7871530/) concluded that creatine without resistance training is unlikely to produce substantial improvements in muscle strength and functional performance. Most longer-term studies of creatine alone also failed to demonstrate a lean-mass benefit.

That distinction matters if you are buying gummies for a parent who does not exercise. Easier administration does not remove the training requirement from the evidence. If illness, pain, balance problems, or recent surgery limits movement, ask a qualified clinician or physical therapist what activity is appropriate instead of replacing rehabilitation with a supplement.

## Creatine and kidney function over 60: what should you ask?

**Kidney-test interpretation and individual kidney safety are separate questions. Tell the clinician ordering blood tests about creatine use; never dismiss an abnormal result yourself.**

A [2025 kidney-function systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41199218/) found a modest increase in serum creatinine but no significant change in glomerular filtration rate compared with control. Creatinine is a blood marker used in assessing kidney function; its interpretation can be affected by creatine use.

These findings do not prove that every person with chronic kidney disease can take creatine safely. They also do not prove the safety of every multi-ingredient gummy. If you have kidney disease, reduced kidney function, unexplained abnormal laboratory results, or a clinician-directed fluid plan, discuss the product before starting.

Bring a list of prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, and supplements. Ask whether baseline testing or follow-up is appropriate for you. Do not stop prescribed medication, change a fluid restriction, or assume that an increase in creatinine is harmless because you read a general review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is creatine safe for seniors?

Age alone does not answer that question. The [2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7871530/) describes generally reassuring evidence at studied intakes in healthy people, including research involving older adults. Existing kidney disease, current medicines, and extra ingredients in the chosen product still require individual review. “Studied in older adults” does not mean “cleared for everyone over 60.”

### How much creatine do older adults take?

Research protocols differ; there is no universal gummy count for everyone over 60. The [2021 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7871530/) discusses lower daily supplementation strategies and explains that a loading phase is not required. Ask a clinician to interpret the evidence alongside your medical history, then follow the actual product label. Do not copy a high-intake study protocol or combine multiple creatine products on your own.

### Can creatine gummies prevent muscle loss without exercise?

Do not rely on them to do so. The [evidence review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7871530/) found that creatine alone was unlikely to generate substantial strength and functional improvements. Gummies are a delivery format, not a replacement for appropriate resistance exercise or evaluation of unexplained weakness and weight loss.

### Are the findings different for older men and women?

The older-adult literature includes both men and women, but no single pooled result predicts an individual response.
