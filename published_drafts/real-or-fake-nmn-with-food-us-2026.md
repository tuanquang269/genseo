## Why do shoppers ask whether NMN is real or fake?

“Fake NMN” can describe several different problems: a counterfeit bottle, a listing that does not match the product it ships, an incomplete label, or a genuine product whose contents have never been independently tested by the shopper. These are not interchangeable findings.

A front label can look convincing while the seller, lot code, seal, or Supplement Facts panel raises questions. The reverse is also possible: packaging can change during a legitimate update. A different bottle color or redesigned logo is a reason to pause and compare, not proof of counterfeiting.

The [FDA Dietary Supplements](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements) page explains that dietary supplements must not be adulterated or misbranded, and that manufacturers and distributors have responsibilities for safety and labeling before marketing. That regulatory framework helps explain why label accuracy matters. It is not a home method for measuring NMN identity, concentration, or contamination.

## What should a US shopper check on an NMN panel?

Read the Supplement Facts panel before trusting a marketplace title, influencer caption, or large number on the front. Check these six points in order.

1. **Product identity:** The name on the listing, bottle, checkout page, and receipt should describe the same product. A seller using one brand name while shipping another bottle is a material mismatch.
2. **Serving size:** Confirm how many capsules make one serving. Do not assume one capsule equals the amount shown in a headline.
3. **Ingredient names and amounts:** Look for named ingredients and declared amounts rather than an unexplained proprietary blend. The panel should make clear what the amount applies to.
4. **Container count:** Compare the capsule count with the serving size and the number of servings. Arithmetic can reveal a listing that quietly changes the package definition.
5. **Lot and expiry information:** A lot code and expiration or best-by information help connect the physical bottle to a seller’s records. Missing or altered information deserves a pause.
6. **Seller and seal:** Check who sold the product, whether the seal is intact, and whether the seller can answer a traceability question. A low price alone does not prove a product is fake, but an unidentified seller plus broken packaging is a stronger warning.

A photograph, smell, capsule color, or taste cannot quantify NMN. Do not open a questionable bottle merely to inspect it if the seal or seller information already concerns you. Photograph the unopened package, receipt, seal, lot code, and listing, then contact the seller.

## How do NMN listings go wrong without proving a fake product?

Listings can become confusing through copied product titles, stale photographs, marketplace substitutions, bundle pages, or a seller using a brand keyword for a different item. A listing may also present the amount per serving as though it were the amount per capsule. That is a communication error unless the physical panel itself makes the same claim.

Compare the product title, package photograph, Supplement Facts panel, serving size, capsule count, price, and seller name as one set. Save a screenshot or PDF of the listing at purchase. If the delivered item differs, keep the packaging and avoid making a potency judgment from the capsule’s appearance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you tell whether NMN is real by looking at the capsules?

No. Capsule color, shape, smell, and firmness do not establish NMN identity, concentration, purity, or contamination. Compare the label and preserve the package; laboratory analysis is needed to measure the contents.

### What should the NMN Supplement Facts panel name?

It should identify the ingredients and state the amount per defined serving. Check the serving size first, then compare the ingredient names and amounts with the current product page. Do not treat a front-label number as an amount per capsule unless the panel says so.

### Does a matching label prove that an NMN bottle is authentic?

No. A counterfeit can copy a label. A matching panel supports consistency but does not establish the supply chain or potency. Seller identity, seal, lot information, receipt, and traceable documentation add context; none replaces suitable testing.
