## Check 2: Which kidney tests should you discuss in the US?

**Ask whether blood creatinine with estimated GFR and a urine albumin test are appropriate for your circumstances.** These are kidney-assessment tools, not a turkesterone safety certificate or a universal testing schedule.

The [National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/tests-diagnosis) explains that a blood test estimates glomerular filtration rate, or eGFR, while urine testing looks for albumin. Albumin is a blood protein that can enter urine when kidneys are damaged. A urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, abbreviated UACR, measures albumin relative to creatinine.

A useful appointment question is: “Given my history and everything I take, do I need kidney testing before considering this supplement?” If testing is indicated, ask who will review the results and what would change the recommendation. Avoid ordering a panel simply to find one green result that seems to justify starting.

| Check | What it can help assess | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Blood creatinine and estimated GFR | Kidney filtration, interpreted by a clinician | That a particular supplement will remain safe |
| Urine albumin or UACR | Albumin leakage that may indicate kidney damage | The cause of an abnormal result by itself |
| Complete product label and medicine list | What exposures need review | Absence of harm simply because ingredients are listed |
| Liver blood tests when indicated | Patterns that may suggest liver problems | A diagnosis from one isolated result |

## Check 3: What about liver tests and stopping?

**Discuss liver concerns separately rather than assuming a kidney result covers both organs.** A clinician can decide whether liver blood tests are indicated and interpret them alongside your history and symptoms.

[MedlinePlus explains liver function tests](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/liver-function-tests/), including ALT, AST and bilirubin. The tests help assess different aspects of liver health, but a panel alone usually cannot identify a specific disease. Abnormal findings can have more than one explanation and may require additional assessment.

Do not start turkesterone while an unexplained abnormal test is still being evaluated. If you suspect a supplement is causing a reaction, stop taking it and contact a healthcare professional promptly rather than continuing until a planned checkup. The [FDA's supplement guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/information-consumers-using-dietary-supplements/questions-and-answers-dietary-supplements) advises contacting a healthcare provider immediately for suspected supplement-related harm.

Yellowing of the eyes or skin and unusually dark urine are among the symptoms MedlinePlus lists in its liver-testing guidance. They warrant medical attention, not a self-directed “detox” or a switch to another supplement. Severe symptoms require urgent care.
