<h2>Energy is the result buyers repeat most</h2>
<p>Thirty of the 85 reviewers describe an energy change, and the phrasing is unusually consistent: steady rather than sharp. "Shockwave is palatable and gives me some quick, gentle energy," writes Karen W. Robert H. describes it as "clean and sustained." Rossemarie K., comparing brands, says this one "has the energy benefit that other brands don't."</p>
<p>The most concrete account comes from a factory worker, George P., who writes that he has "the energy to work twelve hours at my job in a factory." Michael J. puts it plainly: "This stuff has given me more energy throughout the day."</p>
<p>That pattern is worth reading carefully rather than taking at face value. Shilajit's best-known human trial is a 90-day randomised controlled study in which purified shilajit raised total testosterone in men aged 45 to 55 (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26395129/">Pandit et al., 2016</a>). A second controlled trial found better retention of maximal muscle strength over eight weeks of training (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30728074/">Keller et al., 2019</a>). Neither measured "energy" as reviewers use the word. The fatigue research most often cited alongside shilajit is a rat model of chronic fatigue syndrome (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22771318/">Surapaneni et al., 2012</a>) — a legitimate starting point, not a human result.</p>
<p>So the honest summary is that a lot of buyers report more daytime energy, and the controlled human evidence points at adjacent outcomes rather than that one directly. One reviewer's line about his testosterone rising is a self-report, not a lab result. We break down which men's claims have real trial support in <a href="https://wellnessnest.co/blogs/news/shilajit-benefits-for-men-4-claims-ranked-us-2026">shilajit benefits for men</a>.</p>
