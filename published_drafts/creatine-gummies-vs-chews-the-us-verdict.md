## Does creatine stability research prove one format is better?

No. The stability research reviewed here does not establish a general gummy-versus-chew winner. It explains why finished-product evidence matters, not why one shopping category must fail.

A [review of creatine formulations and stability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3080578/) describes creatine monohydrate as stable in dry powder while reporting conversion to creatinine in aqueous solutions under some storage conditions. Temperature and pH influence that process. These observations concern particular conditions; they are not measurements of the gummy or chew in your basket.

There are two tempting but unsupported shortcuts. One is to assume every chew behaves like dry powder. The other is to take a loss measured in an acidic solution and apply the same percentage to every gummy. Neither follows from the evidence reviewed.

Instead, request evidence that corresponds to the finished formulation and its storage instructions. A report from soon after manufacturing answers a different question from a stability program following the product over time. Both can be informative, but they should not be described as interchangeable.

When reading a report, ask what was measured and when. A document about ingredient identity does not by itself establish finished-product content at expiry. Likewise, a contaminant screen should not be presented as if it automatically measures the amount of creatine remaining. Read the named tests and results rather than treating “tested” as a complete answer.

## What does human creatine research actually support?

Human research supports creatine monohydrate for certain exercise outcomes; it does not make every product containing the ingredient equivalent. The [International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/) reviews evidence for high-intensity exercise performance and training adaptations, including strength and lean-mass outcomes.

The same review explains the underlying energy role: phosphocreatine helps regenerate ATP during rapid energy demands. That mechanism is about creatine physiology. It is not proof that chewing longer, choosing a softer texture or buying a particular brand improves the mechanism.

Neither that position stand nor the stability review establishes a head-to-head performance advantage for gummies over chews. This is a statement about the sources used here, not a claim that every study ever published has been searched. Keep ingredient evidence, product-content evidence and comparative human evidence separate.

Safety deserves the same restraint. The position stand describes generally good tolerability in the studied settings and identifies weight gain as a consistent reported effect. It advises people with pre-existing kidney disease to consult a physician. Those findings should not be converted into a promise that every multi-ingredient gummy or chew suits every person.

If you have a health condition, take medication or are unsure whether supplementation is appropriate, discuss the complete label with a qualified clinician. Do not substitute a format comparison for an individual assessment.
