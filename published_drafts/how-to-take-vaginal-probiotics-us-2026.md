## 4. Handle a missed day without doubling

If you miss a day, return to the label-directed serving at the next practical opportunity. Do not automatically double the next serving to “catch up.” If you are unsure after missing several days, ask a pharmacist or clinician rather than improvising a schedule.

![Hand restarting a FloraFresh routine beside water on a bedroom side table](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0810/1563/6283/files/missed-day.webp?v=1792464565)

Travel makes missed days more likely. Pack the product according to its storage instructions, keep the pouch closed, and set a reminder tied to an existing meal or hygiene habit. A missed day is a routine problem, not a reason to make a new dose plan.

## 5. Know when a supplement is not the answer

A probiotic supplement does not diagnose or treat an infection. New, persistent, painful, or unusual symptoms deserve medical advice, especially with fever, pelvic pain, pregnancy, recurrent symptoms, or symptoms after sexual exposure. Seek urgent care for severe pain, fever, fainting, or other emergency signs.

The FDA explains that dietary supplements are regulated as foods rather than drugs and that product claims must not be confused with approval to treat disease: [FDA dietary supplement guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements). For general background on probiotics, the [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements probiotic fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Probiotics-HealthProfessional/) is a useful starting point, though the page may restrict automated requests.
