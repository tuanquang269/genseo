NMN vs collagen is a choice between different research targets, not interchangeable anti-aging supplements. For shoppers in the US, collagen has more direct skin-outcome research, but study quality weakens the promise. NMN studies examine NAD and other outcomes; they do not establish a skin benefit. Choose by the evidence relevant to your goal, not the advertising category.

## What does NMN research actually show?

**NMN has human research on blood NAD and selected health measures; that is not the same as evidence that it reduces wrinkles.** In a [randomized trial of NMN](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36482258/), researchers measured blood NAD concentrations, walking performance and other outcomes. The trial was not a collagen comparison or a test of a retail NMN-and-TMG blend.

That distinction matters when a product page uses broad language about healthy aging. A measurable change in a blood marker answers a narrower question than whether somebody looks younger, lives longer or experiences a meaningful improvement in daily life. Readers should not treat those outcomes as synonyms.

The trial enrolled **80 middle-aged adults** and ran for **60 days**. Blood NAD increased in the NMN groups compared with placebo, and researchers reported improvements in walking-test performance. They also reported that supplementation was well tolerated during the trial. Those findings belong to that study, its participants and its observation period—not every supplement with NMN on its label. [Trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36482258/).

Broader metabolic claims deserve particular restraint. A [systematic review of eight randomized trials involving 342 adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39531138/) found no significant pooled benefit on the glucose and lipid measures it examined. This does not erase every positive individual trial. It does mean that selecting one encouraging result while ignoring the pooled findings gives an incomplete buying picture.

**NMN's practical advantage:** a defined research question about NAD-related outcomes. **Its limitation:** that research cannot be repackaged as demonstrated wrinkle reduction or a universal wellness result. If skin appearance is your only goal, ask for a skin study before paying for a different endpoint.

For a separate formulation decision, see [NMN alone versus NMN plus TMG](https://wellnessnest.co/blogs/news/nmn-alone-vs-nmn-tmg-which-fits-your-goal). The distinction between a single ingredient and a combination is important even before collagen enters the comparison.

## What does collagen research say about skin?

**Collagen research more directly addresses skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles, but its results are not uniformly persuasive.** Two published reviews illustrate why an attractive pooled result is not the whole answer.

A [review of collagen-based supplements](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40826844/) pooled **ten randomized trials with 646 participants** and reported improvements in hydration and elasticity. Its authors also identified limitations: unclear risk of bias, differences between studies and results more applicable to women. They could not assess publication bias because the evidence set was small.

A different [2025 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40324552/) analyzed **23 randomized trials with 1,474 participants**. The overall analysis favored collagen for hydration, elasticity and wrinkles. However, the benefits were not significant in the higher-quality studies or the subgroup without pharmaceutical-company funding. The authors concluded that the evidence did not support collagen supplements for preventing or treating skin aging.

These reviews do not ask readers to choose a preferred headline. They show why study selection, funding and quality need to be read alongside the result. “More studies” can mean a better-targeted research base without meaning that the answer is settled.

**Collagen's practical advantage:** the outcomes studied are closer to the question a skin-focused shopper is asking. **Its limitation:** a confident promise of visible rejuvenation outruns the quality-sensitive findings. A shopper can reasonably decide that this uncertainty is not worth the cost.

For this comparison, collagen means the supplement category studied in those reviews, not a particular branded product. A named formula would require its own ingredient and study checks rather than borrowing the whole category's reputation.
