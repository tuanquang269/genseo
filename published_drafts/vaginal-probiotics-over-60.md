## What changes after menopause—and what needs assessment?

**Menopause can change the vaginal environment, but a change does not automatically mean you need a probiotic.** A [review of the genitourinary microbiome after menopause](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10047399/) describes falling estrogen, reduced vaginal Lactobacillus abundance and higher vaginal pH. That biological background does not establish that an oral gummy reverses those changes.

Start with the concern you actually have: dryness, discomfort, a new discharge, or simply uncertainty about a supplement. Do not let the phrase “vaginal balance” replace a description of your symptoms. A clinician can assess the problem; a product category cannot diagnose it.

The [NHS vaginal-dryness guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vaginal-dryness/) identifies menopause as one possible cause, alongside certain medicines and other conditions. It recommends seeking medical advice for dryness lasting several weeks despite self-care or affecting daily life. Unusual discharge and bleeding after sex or after menopause also warrant assessment. **Do not wait for a probiotic trial to finish before reporting postmenopausal bleeding.**

## Did the oral-probiotic trial answer an over-60 question?

**Postmenopausal research is not automatically evidence specific to women over 60.** The [Petricevic randomized trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18701205/) reports 72 postmenopausal participants and a 14-day intervention. It tested oral Lactobacillus rhamnosus GR-1 and Lactobacillus reuteri RC-14 against placebo. Its primary outcome was a change of at least two grades in the Nugent score, a vaginal-swab measure—not a general promise of better comfort.

The abstract reports improvement in that score more often in the probiotic group. However, the abstract retrieved for this article does not give participants' ages or a separate result for those over 60. It therefore cannot confirm how many participants were over 60, or whether that subgroup benefited. Calling this a dedicated “over-60 trial” would overstate the available information.

A useful evidence check asks three separate questions:

- **Population:** Were people your age actually enrolled, rather than merely eligible?
- **Intervention:** Was it the same strain and an oral product, rather than a vaginal preparation or combined treatment?
- **Outcome:** Did researchers measure symptoms you care about, a laboratory score, or something else?

These distinctions matter when comparing vaginal probiotics for older women. A study can be encouraging without answering your shopping question. Neither the trial abstract nor the review establishes that the FloraFresh formula produces the same result.
