## What does ashwagandha research actually show?

**Ashwagandha is the more relevant research lead when stress or sleep is your main concern.** The [National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha) says some preparations may help stress and insomnia, while evidence for anxiety remains unclear. That is a qualified statement about certain preparations, not an endorsement of every supplement bearing the name.

One [randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled root-extract study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23439798/) enrolled 64 adults with a history of chronic stress. At 60 days, the extract group had lower stress-assessment scores and serum cortisol than the placebo group. The study was conducted at a single center, and the abstract reported mild adverse effects comparable between groups, with no serious adverse events reported.

Those details help explain both the interest and the limits. The study supports further consideration of a particular root extract for a particular outcome. It does not prove that an unspecified gummy, mixed herbal formula, or different extract will reproduce the result. A small trial without serious events also cannot exclude uncommon harms.

If you are comparing products, record the plant part and extract description instead of looking only at the word “ashwagandha.” Ask whether the manufacturer's cited research applies to that preparation. A label with more impressive-looking numbers is not automatically the one with better evidence for your goal.
