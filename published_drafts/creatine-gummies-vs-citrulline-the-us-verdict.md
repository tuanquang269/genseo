## Citrulline: what do repetition studies actually show?

**Citrulline malate may offer a small acute repetition benefit, but evidence for maximal strength is less encouraging.** Read the outcome before reading the marketing conclusion.

The [repetition-performance meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34010809/) included eight studies and reported an average improvement of approximately three repetitions across the exercise protocols. The protocols involved multiple sets; this does **not** mean three extra repetitions on every set. The analysis described the overall effect as small, with variation between studies.

The included studies used **6–8 g of citrulline malate roughly 40–60 minutes before exercise**. These are research conditions, not personalized instructions and not a recommended dose for FusiForce. They also should not be copied onto a label for pure L-citrulline: the named material is different, so an identical gram number is not an established equivalence.

A separate [systematic review of muscle-strength outcomes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34176406/) found no overall strength benefit from citrulline malate in the resistance-trained participants it analyzed. The two reviews are not necessarily contradictory. Repetitions before fatigue and maximal strength answer different questions.
