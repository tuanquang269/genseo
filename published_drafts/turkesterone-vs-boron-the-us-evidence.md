## Does boron improve strength or testosterone?

**The boron evidence reviewed here differs by outcome: a bodybuilding trial found no added training benefit, while a small short-term study reported hormone changes.** Neither finding should be hidden to make the comparison easier.

The [bodybuilding trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8508192/) enrolled nineteen males aged twenty to twenty-seven. Ten received boron and nine received placebo during seven weeks. Researchers measured total and free testosterone, plasma boron, lean body mass, and strength at the beginning and end.

Boron supplementation had no significant effect on the dependent variables. Both groups improved in total testosterone, lean body mass, squat strength, and bench-press strength. The authors concluded that bodybuilding could improve those measures in lesser-trained participants, but the boron supplement did not add an effect in this experiment.

For someone shopping specifically for a stronger squat or more muscle, that is directly relevant negative evidence. It is not proof about every possible population or protocol. It is still a reason not to advertise boron as an established muscle-building supplement on the strength of this trial.

### Why the testosterone headline is incomplete

A separate [short-term boron study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21129941/) involved eight healthy male volunteers. After one week, the investigators reported increased mean free testosterone and decreased mean estradiol in the morning samples. That finding helps explain the attention boron receives in hormone discussions.

But the study was small and short. Its abstract describes hormone and inflammatory measurements, not a demonstration of added muscle growth or improved lifting performance. It does not overturn the bodybuilding trial simply because its hormone findings sound encouraging.

The defensible answer to “turkesterone or boron for testosterone?” is therefore limited. These sources do not supply a head-to-head comparison, and a short-term hormone result is not a treatment recommendation. If a laboratory result or persistent symptom concerns you, discuss it with a qualified healthcare professional rather than choosing a supplement from a headline.
