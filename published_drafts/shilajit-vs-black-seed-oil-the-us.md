**Shilajit is a complex natural material; black seed products come from Nigella sativa.** Their research histories and constituent profiles are different. Neither name tells you whether a particular bottle or jar matches the preparation used in a clinical trial.

## What does the shilajit research actually show?

**A placebo-controlled study reported changes in hormone measurements after purified shilajit, but it did not compare shilajit with black seed oil.** That is narrower than proving better energy, better workouts or a general advantage over another supplement.

After 90 days, researchers reported significantly higher total testosterone, free testosterone and DHEAS compared with placebo. ## What does the black seed evidence show?

**Nigella sativa has been studied across several clinical outcomes, but the quality of the evidence is a major limitation.** More published topics do not necessarily mean more dependable answers for a healthy shopper.

The [overview of systematic reviews and meta-analyses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10086143/) included 20 meta-analyses. Its assessment classified only one as moderate methodological quality, four as low quality and 15 as critically low quality. Among 110 outcome indicators, five had moderate-quality evidence, 17 low-quality evidence and 88 very-low-quality evidence.

Those numbers describe the reviewed literature, not the probability that a bottle will help you. The authors identified problems including risk of bias, inconsistency and imprecision. They called for better clinical trials rather than treating the existing results as a final answer.

A [later meta-analysis in people with metabolic diseases](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41858302/) reported modest improvements in some cardiovascular risk markers, with no significant effects on some other measures. Its authors also advised caution because of variation in study quality and outcomes. Results from those patient groups should not become a recommendation for healthy readers to self-treat or replace prescribed care.

This is the practical black seed oil distinction: there is a broader range of research questions to examine, but the evidence still needs sorting by preparation, participant group and endpoint. “Studied for” is not the same phrase as “established to work for.”
