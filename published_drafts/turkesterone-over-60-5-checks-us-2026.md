For context, the older-adult evidence found for ingredients in this category is ingredient-specific. A randomized crossover trial in men aged 40–70 studied a particular Shoden ashwagandha preparation for eight weeks at a time. The trial did not establish a benefit for every ashwagandha extract, and it did not test this formula. Neither study answers the turkesterone-over-60 question.

## 3. Could the ingredients conflict with medicines or conditions?

**Ask a physician or pharmacist to review the complete label before use if you take medicines or have a chronic condition.** This is especially important for thyroid disease, diabetes, blood-pressure treatment, liver disease, or a complicated medication list. Do not stop a prescribed medicine to try a supplement.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health says evidence is insufficient to establish long-term ashwagandha safety, and it is not recommended for people with thyroid or autoimmune disorders. NCCIH also notes reports linking ashwagandha supplements with liver injury and possible interactions with medicines for diabetes and high blood pressure ([NCCIH safety guidance](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)). Tongkat ali adds a separate uncertainty: the older-men trial tested one standardized extract in a defined population for a limited period. It cannot establish long-term safety for another extract or for people with different health conditions. Black pepper extract may also matter when a pharmacist reviews the whole supplement list, because the question is about combinations, not one ingredient in isolation.

Use this five-minute check before buying:

1. List every prescription medicine, over-the-counter medicine, and supplement.
