Human trials of purified shilajit have used **250 mg to 500 mg per day**, taken as one or two doses. That is the studied band for US adults. But the number on your jar or pouch will not look like it, because resin, gummies and extract powders are measured on completely different scales — so the practical answer is your own label, read correctly.

## The Studied Range: 250–500 mg Per Day

Three human trials define the range most US brands quote, and all three used a *purified, standardised* preparation rather than raw material.

- **250 mg twice daily (500 mg/day), 90 days.** In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in men aged 45–55, purified shilajit at this dose raised total testosterone, free testosterone and DHEAS versus placebo ([Pandit et al., *Andrologia*, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26395129/)).
- **250 mg/day vs 500 mg/day, 8 weeks.** In 63 recreationally active men, only the 500 mg group retained maximal isometric strength after a fatiguing protocol and showed lower serum hydroxyproline ([Keller et al., *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12970-019-0270-2)).
- **100 mg twice daily (200 mg/day), 90 days.** A smaller trial in oligospermic men reported improvements in sperm count, motility and serum testosterone at a lower dose ([Biswas et al., *Andrologia*, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20078516/)).

Two things fall out of that. Higher was better in the one trial that compared doses head to head — 250 mg/day did not reproduce the strength result 500 mg/day did. And every one of these numbers describes *purified* material. A safety review of the toxicology and clinical literature concluded that processed, purified shilajit is safe at standard supplement doses, while raw unprocessed material can carry heavy metals above acceptable limits ([Stohs, *Phytotherapy Research*, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23733436/)). Dose and purification are not separate questions.

## Why the Form Changes the Number

A milligram of resin, a milligram of a 10:1 extract and a milligram of gummy base are not the same amount of shilajit. That is the single reason "how much shilajit per day" has no universal answer.

- **Resin** is the concentrated material itself, softened and purified. Its serving is a volume you eyeball — which is where most dosing errors start.
- **Gummies** carry a *standardised extract*, usually expressed as a ratio. A 10:1 extract means one part extract was concentrated from ten parts raw material, so the label often shows both figures and readers compare the wrong one.
- **Drops and blends** dilute shilajit into a carrier alongside other actives, so the shilajit fraction is a subset of the total serving.

Comparing your product to a 500 mg trial dose only works if you are comparing the same kind of milligram.

## When to Take It, and How Long Before You Judge It

Timing matters far less than consistency. None of the trials above tested morning versus evening; they tested a daily amount held for 8 to 13 weeks. Two of the three ran a full 90 days before measuring the outcome.

That is the number worth internalising. A one-week trial of any shilajit format tells you almost nothing except whether your stomach tolerates it. Split dosing — half in the morning, half in the evening — is what the 250 mg twice-daily trials actually did, so it is a defensible pattern if your label's serving divides cleanly. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much shilajit per day is safe for an adult in the US?

Published human trials used 200–500 mg per day of purified shilajit for 8–13 weeks without safety signals, and a review of the toxicology literature concluded purified shilajit is safe at standard supplement doses ([Stohs, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23733436/)). Your product's labeled serving is the operative limit — do not exceed it. Talk to a healthcare professional first if you take medication, are pregnant or nursing, or have a medical condition.

### Is a pea-sized portion of resin too much?

Usually, yes. Running the numbers on a 30 g / 75-serving jar gives about 400 mg per serving, which is closer to a grain of rice than a pea. "Pea-sized" is loose shorthand that most people round upward. Weigh or eyeball against the arithmetic on your own package instead.

### Can I take shilajit gummies and resin on the same day?

We do not recommend stacking formats to chase a milligram target you read online. Each label is written for that product used on its own, and doubling up puts you past the labeled serving of both. Pick one format and stay with it long enough to judge it.

### How long until I notice anything?

The trials measured outcomes at 8 weeks (strength) and 90 days (hormonal and fertility markers). Plan on a minimum of eight consistent weeks before you decide whether a format is doing anything for you.

### Does more shilajit work better?

Not without a ceiling.
